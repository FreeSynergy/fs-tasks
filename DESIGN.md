# fs-tasks – Design

`fs-tasks` is the tasks program of FreeSynergy and the **owner of the JSCalendar `Task`** (ADR 0050: one standard, one
owning program, one interface). This repo holds the **program core** (`crates/fs-tasks-core`, no GUI), the server (`crates/fs-tasks-server`) and the **frontend** (`crates/fs-tasks-app`, see below). The contract
(`tasks.capnp`) lives in [`fs-interfaces`](https://github.com/FreeSynergy/fs-interfaces) (ADR 0045); this program is its owner. Other programs, e.g. `fs-calendar`, use tasks **only through the interface** (`TaskItems`, `TaskFeed`) and have
no Task model of their own. Design of the whole program: `fs-documentation/docs/en/programs/apps/tasks/design.md`.

## Interface status

**Interface status: draft, consumers: fs-calendar (1 of 2).** (ADR 0045: the interface is a target. It stays draft until two
independent consumers have tested it, then 1.0, then only version jumps.) The contract is `tasks.capnp` in `fs-interfaces` (marked DRAFT; owner: this program). The Rust form comes first (ADR 0043): the traits
`TaskItems` and `TaskFeed`.

Now, 5000 tasks: **release** import 1.8 s, readiness of all 5000 at once 0.47 s, readiness of the head 0.20 s, **`ready` page of 10: 0.22 s**, **`relate` in front of the chain: 19 ms**, `relate` closing a cycle through all 5000 (the way round is named in the error): 0.36 s; **debug** (the same test) 34 s import, 7.8 s for all 5000, 2.3 s for the head, 2.1 s for the `ready` page, 0.30 s for `relate` in front, 5.0 s for the cycle. What changed: a task is judged against a graph loaded once (few tasks: exact index lookups followed a few levels; many tasks or a deep graph: one scan of the store, which costs the same however deep the graph is), the edges are indexed by their successor, cycles are found once per call, the `ready` page is one scan plus one linear judgement (no per-task walk), and `relate` searches from both ends with a small budget of lookups (a new task in front of a chain has nothing behind it and is answered at once; only a possible cycle reads the whole graph). Limits (`TaskRepo::with_graph_limits`, tests with lowered values in `tests/graph_limits.rs`): a question that follows the graph (one task, many tasks, `relate` after its quick search, the check after a change) loads at most 20 000 tasks, else it is refused with a conflict that names the limit; a scan reads at most 100 000 tasks of the store (also refused with a conflict); the scan path of the `ready` filter checks the first limit on the tasks it parses (those that hold a dependency and those they name), not on the whole store. A scan goes on from the last **`published`** time it saw (`published >= last`, the tasks of that instant already seen are skipped; an edit does not change `published`), not by a count of rows and not by an index: an edit made during the scan neither hides a task nor shows one twice (`tests/scan_stable.rs` edits between two of its queries), a task created during the scan comes at its end, and nothing depends on index fields in the ordinary case; **when more tasks than one chunk share one `published` instant** (a store written with given times) that instant is read on its own by task id (the `id_key` index, over the rows of that one instant only) and the scan goes on after it, a full chunk without progress is never taken for the end (`tests/scan_ties.rs`; red under `>=` instead of `>` and with the tie reading taken out); a tie instant whose tasks lack the id key (written before 0.7.0) is refused with the hint to run `upgrade_index` (a first version continued by the `id_key` index and was 25 times slower: an index condition is a pass over the rows each time, 5 s instead of 0.2 s for 5000 tasks in a release build, found by the review). **A task the store does not hold** (named by a relation, or gone) is a node without relations of its own, but the tasks that depend on it are followed, so a cycle through it is a conflict by every way of asking (`tests/readiness_equiv.rs`, random graphs with such names). `relate` does **not** see a cycle that runs through a task the store does not hold (it never did); the cycle shows as a conflict once it is there. The change check (`Unblocked`) loads the graph around the changed task once and judges before (the task as it was) and after, so it also covers a task that is **created or imported** (a task that named it as a dependency may be free now: a few created tasks are asked one by one through the index, many by one scan of the graph; `tests/unblocked_created.rs`), and it costs a closure read when a progress changes, a relation is taken away or a task is deleted (not for a title, a label, a relation that is only added or whose gap changes: those cannot free anybody; `relate` in front of a long chain stays at about 14 ms). **It runs after the write turn is given back** (`TaskRepo::writing`: the write only queues the change; the judgement is done before the call returns, but without the turn), so no other write waits for it (`tests/write_turn.rs` slows the queries and shows a create finishing before the edit that is still judging). Measured (release, 5000-chain, the machine's load average was 14 during the measurement, three runs agree within 10 %): a title edit 2.4 ms, completing the head 0.25 s (the judgement), all other numbers above. **What is judged is the store as it is when the judgement runs**: "before" is the task as it was, "after" is read from the store, so of two writers the later one decides (a completion judged while another writer reopens the task announces nothing; `tests/unblocked_race.rs`, red under a mutation that takes a stale "after"). A task announced as ready is remembered until a later judgement sees it waiting, so two writers that free the same task at once announce it once (`tests/unblocked_race.rs`; the memory is cleared at 10 000 entries, at worst a task is announced twice). Judgements run one at a time (`flush_turn`); a call that queued a change returns only after it was judged, by itself or by the judgement that took it (the next one waits for that one). The second guarantee is by construction; the test (`a_call_returns_only_after_its_own_announcement_was_sent`) is a smoke test and stays green when the mutex is taken out, because the window between giving back the write turn and the flush cannot be hit deterministically from outside.  the `ready` page is cut from the in-memory order `(order key, lower-case name, id)`, so a place taken from an unfiltered page is found by its task, and by that key if the task is gone. ## Frontend (`crates/fs-tasks-app`, provisional)

A list and a view by status of one set of tasks; search; create, edit (title, description, due, priority, status, labels), delete; a card moved by keyboard, by the buttons beside the task, or by a frontend's gesture through `TasksWidget::grab`, `hover`, `drop_held`; iCalendar in and out by path on the command line (`--store`, `--import`, `--export`, `--export-sensitive`, `--snapshot`, `--size`, `--view list|status`). It imports no `blinc_*` crate. Its menus (ADR 0056, only real commands, in `surface.rs`): Task: New; View: List, Status; the compact variant has none. The work surface is the workbench `tasks` in `fs-components`, which knows no store and no type of this repo and has no window function: it gives its host a plain context (selected key, view; ADR 0056).

**In process, provisional.** `TaskAdapter` drives `Arc<dyn TaskItems>` with a tokio handle from the interface thread, until the RPC client of `fs-tasks-server` exists. `TaskSource` is synchronous, so a slow core freezes the window.

**Provisional decision: the status view is not a board.** The design says boards, columns and `task_link` are proposals with no standard, and that moving a card on a board must not change the task for everyone. **Real boards are open and not built here: no new standard, no `Boards` interface.** This first view is a view over the task's own JSCalendar `progress`, one column per value, and it is called "Status" in the GUI, not board or kanban. So moving a card **is an edit of the task's own status, for everyone**, and the screen says so: always above the columns, also that a status worked out from the participants becomes fixed on a move and cannot go back to that in the GUI, and after each move.

| What a move does | |
|---|---|
| Writes | `progress` (the value the person chose) and `progressUpdated`; the core sets `updated` |
| Keeps | everything else, compared as JSON in `tests/moves.rs`: participants and what they said, `percentComplete`, `freesynergy.net:taskStatus`, relations, `extra`, the original iCalendar |
| To the status the task effectively has | writes nothing, so a status the core only computes (`effective_progress`) stays computed. An edit that leaves the status alone does not write it either |
| A task that repeats, or one occurrence | refused with the reason, in the source and on the card: its status belongs to each occurrence, and there is no per-occurrence edit yet. Its form has no due date and no status; title, description, priority and labels change for all occurrences |
| From an old row | a conflict; nothing is written and what is stored now is shown |

**`Unsupported` in the frontend:** own boards, columns other than the five values of `progress`, order within a column (it follows the list: by due date, a card put elsewhere goes back and this is said), swimlanes, column limits, assigning people, relations, `task_link`, checklists, timeline, comments, attachments, reminders, undo, recurrence and per-occurrence edits. What the form does not show stays on the task and is shown on the sheet where it is simple (participants and related tasks by count, percent, extended status). The picker dialogs for import and export do not exist: paths on the command line.

**Sensitive tasks (ADR 0029).** Marked in list, card, sheet and form; never opened by the workbench; `--export` leaves them out and names them by `uid` alone, `--export-sensitive <uid>` releases one, and a file with a released task is mode 0600. **The core has no check of its own:** `is_marked` walks the task for the member `freesynergy.net:sensitive`, also as a patch path in an override, and for `privacy` other than `public`, and reads `envelope.sensitive`; a mention in a text or a key of an id map (a label called `privacy`) marks nothing. To be replaced by the core's. The core also does not fill `envelope.sensitive` from the vendor property, as `fs-people-core` does, so the store's refusal to keep marked objects unencrypted does not apply to tasks marked that way.

**Other limits.** A write compares the revision and then writes: not atomic, `TaskItems::edit` takes no revision (a compare-and-set is a wish for the core). `rows` reads the whole list each time and cuts a page from it; `TaskItems::page` and the filters `term` and `open` exist now, the frontend has not moved to them (Thread 4). There is no sign-in, so "my next tasks" (the compact variant) is every open task. Floating tasks are ordered as if in UTC; there is no `--zone` yet. A due date needs a time: "that day" has no form yet. A keyword switched off is kept and not shown; one with a comma stands in quotes in the form. One unreadable stored task makes the whole list and export fail, plainly: `TaskItems::list` answers all or nothing, and the contract has no other way through the tasks (with Thread 3). Closing the window loses an unsaved edit. Provisional words and sizes are in `words.rs`.

## Work board (0.0.8: workers work through fs-tasks)

- **CLI as client.** The command line is a client of the socket only; the new worker commands use `Items.get/list/create/editIf/readiness`, no wire change. `failed` is the JSCalendar `progress` value `failed` that the core always had; the board has its column. Claim, release and status are read-change-`editIf` with a re-read after a lost race (`fs-tasks-cli/src/edit.rs`); the claim rule is the core's (`Task::claimed_at`, `set_claim`, 24 h). Conflict = exit 3, holder in the message.
- **Members** (`model/work.rs`, vendor `freesynergy.net:*`, kept verbatim): `origin` (`local` | `imported`), `createdBy`, `check`, `checked` (`{command, at}`), `note` (500 bytes, checked by `validate`). `import_ics` writes `origin: imported` and drops `checked`, whatever the text said.
- **Check = code** (`fs-tasks-cli/src/policy.rs`, `exec.rs`, `check.rs`): refusal order origin, creator (trust list), program (allowlist: `cargo`, `fs-release`, listed names, `bash scripts/<relative, no ..>`, symlinks out of `scripts/` refused), then an argument-vector spawn (no shell) with a time limit and the last 2 KiB of output. `done` of an objective task needs `checked.command == check`. Settings: `check-policy.json` in the data folder; an unreadable file means nothing runs. The wire is the local user's own door: a task made over the wire keeps what it says, so **sync must rewrite `origin` like the import does** (not built).
- **One motor, many faces.** One server (`fs-tasks serve`, its own process and process group) per device holds `<data>/objects.db` (locked by one process). The window, the CLI and agents are clients of its socket. `fs_tasks_server::ensure_server` (double-checked lock file next to the socket) starts it if none answers; CLI and window use the same function, so many clients start one server. The window never opens the data file itself: `fs_tasks_server::RemoteItems` implements `TaskItems` over `tasks.capnp` (the capnp client is not `Send`, so it lives on a thread of its own and calls are jobs sent to it). Window options: `--connect <socket>` picks the server (also for a picture or an export); only `--store <file>`, or a picture/export without `--connect`, keep data in the process (then no server). Closing the window leaves the server running (it ends on a signal); no idle shutdown. The server is not reconnected if it dies: calls then fail with an error, restart the window. Not on the wire, so not available to the window: `claim`, `assign_actor`, `page` (store places) and `readiness_many` in one call (the default asks one by one: slow for thousands of tasks). `fs_tasks_server::host` (serving a repository from a thread) stays for tests only.
- **Board live.** The way is the server's change feed: `Tasks.subscribe` with a `ChangeListener` (not polling). `RemoteItems::changes()` calls registered callbacks on every notice (own changes too); the board face reloads its cache in the background (`Cache::reload_in_background`, coalesced) and redraws, the classic face refreshes its list. Tested at the cache level (a change by another client moves the card to "In progress" within 5 s; a cache without the feed stays stale), not at the screen.
- **Check processes.** On a timeout the check's whole process group is ended (Unix: the child is started with `process_group(0)`, then `kill -s KILL -- -<pgid>`; the `kill` tool is used so no library is added). Windows: only the child is ended; grandchildren may survive (documented limit, untested).
- Secrets: `add`, `note`, `status --note/--report` refuse marks of keys and tokens (`secrets.rs`); a guard against slips, not a scanner.

## Plan views (`fs-tasks-app`, `--plan board|next`, branch `frontends-1`, provisional)

## Paging (`TaskItems::page`, `repo/page.rs`, server `cursor.rs`, `paging.rs`)

The order is the store's `order_by_index("order_key")`: smallest case-folded `order_key`, then the case-folded title, then the id as text. `order_key` is the due time as index text, or `9999-12-31T23:59:60Z` for a task without one, so undated tasks come last and every task has a place. The cursor (form byte 2) holds the three parts of the last task sent: `order_key` as indexed, the envelope name as stored, the id. The next page is at most three store queries, each with a limit, that together give everything after that place: same `order_key` and same folded title with a greater `id_key`; same `order_key` and a greater folded title; a greater `order_key`. All filters are in every query. The comparisons are consistent with the store's order: `order_key` is one fixed shape of text, so raw and folded order agree; the title goes through the store's own folding (`Cond::Name`); the id is compared as text, as the store orders it (index field `id_key`). A task added or removed between two pages moves no boundary. Cursor form 1 (a count) is refused as `invalid-request`.

Measured, not assumed (`fs-tasks-server/tests/counting.rs`): rows the store returns while all pages are read, at n and 2n tasks; a whole list per page gives ratio 4.00, the keyset gives about 2.

| Index field | Value | For |
|---|---|---|
| `order_key` | due time, or the sentinel | the order and the place |
| `id_key` | the object id as text | the last tie-breaker of a place |
| `description` | the description | `term` |

## Problem extensions (`fs-tasks-server/src/facts.rs`)

The detail text is as before; the facts are extensions (`fs_interfaces::rpc::problem_keys`). A client reads them, it does not take the text apart.

| Problem type | Cause | Extensions |
|---|---|---|
| `tasks/not-found` (404) | no task with the id | `id`, `kind` = `task`; a store `NotFound` gives `id` only |
| `tasks/conflict` (409) | `edit_if`/`edit` with a stale revision | `id`, `kind` = `task` |
| `tasks/conflict` (409) | a uid or id that is taken (`Error::Conflict`) | none: the error carries text, not the object (open) |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | JSCalendar rules broken | `issue`, once per issue, in the order found (`path: message`) |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | the text is no JSON / no task | `issue` = the reason |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | a required text is empty | `issue` = the field (`label`, `email`, `otherUid`) |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | a time, duration or zone | `kind` = `time`, `issue` = the reason |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | iCalendar text | `kind` = `icalendar`, `issue` = the reason |
| `tasks/invalid` (422) | the store refused | `issue` = the reason |
| `too-large` (413) | a task over `MAX_TASK_BYTES`, or a text over the intake (`N bytes, at most M fit`) | `size`, `limit` (bytes) |
| `Unsupported` (501), `invalid-request` (422), unreadable stored data (no problem) | | none |

## Decisions

* **Independent of fs-calendar.** `Task` stands on its own (ADR 0050). What it shares with `Event` lives in `fs-jscalendar`, a plain
  library without data or program, so neither program copies it and neither depends on the other for it.
* **Context `tasks`.** Tasks written by fs-calendar 0.1 have `context=calendar`; they are not found by `TaskRepo`. No data
  existed outside tests, so there is no migration.
* **Assign** adds a participant with role `owner`; assigning the same address twice keeps one participant.
* **Relations** use the `Relation` object of JSCalendar (`relatedTo[uid].relation[key] = true`); keys: `first next child parent`
  (RFC 8984) and `depends-on finishtostart finishtofinish starttostart starttofinish` (RFC 9253). Lead/lag gaps are not modelled.
* **Index times are clamped, not refused:** `0000-01-01T00:00:00` in Auckland is valid JSCalendar, so it stays storable. Its index value is the edge of the range and its envelope keeps the real time (provisional).
* **Task envelope type** is `Task` (AS2 has no task type); content type `application/jscalendar+json`.
* **Wire form of a task**: JSCalendar JSON text plus the envelope; the JSON stays authoritative.

## Tags not to use

* **v0.5.0, v0.5.1.** Both pin `fs-interfaces` v0.22.0, whose `rpc` is superseded (see its `DESIGN.md`). In the server: a `due` of 15 MiB of control characters passes intake, its error escapes to more than a message and ends the connection; a report of an import can do the same, and the report is lost; a title of nearly 16 MiB is accepted, and then `list` answers 413 forever, as the title counts twice. In the core: `:60` is taken in any minute; a time near year 0 or 9999 falls out of every range; a `DURATION` raises a false alarm, while garbage in other properties goes unsaid; a continuation line can end a `VTODO`, and data is lost silently (`fs-jscalendar` before 0.1.3).

## Open points (0.7.0)

* A taken uid is `Error::Exists(id)`: the problem carries `id` and `kind` of the task that holds it. `Error::is_conflict` is true for it and for `Conflict`; other conflicts (the uid of a task cannot change) name no object.
* A task stored by 0.6.0 has no `order_key`, `id_key` or `description` in its index. `TaskRepo::upgrade_index` completes them (the server binary runs it at start and prints the count and every failure to stderr); it is idempotent, adds only index fields (content, envelope and original stay; it writes one `Update` per task to the log) and one task that cannot be repaired is named in its report and does not stop the others. Until then, and for a task that cannot be repaired, the first page fails with `stored task cannot be used: N of M tasks have no place in the order ...` (`Error::Stored`) instead of giving fewer tasks; `list` and `query` still return them, keyless ones in name order after the others. A library user that does not use the binary calls `upgrade_index` itself after opening. Tested with a store written by the code of tag v0.6.0 (`tests/fixtures/v060.db`, six tasks).
* The cursor holds the title of the last task whole, with no limit of its own: it is as long as a title may be (up to `MAX_TASK_BYTES`, 16 MiB), and a message takes 64 MiB.
* The store sorts all rows that match before it applies a limit (`ORDER BY` a computed key): a page costs the database O(n log n), so reading all pages is O(n² log n) there. `counting.rs` counts the rows the store *returns*, which are linear; it does not measure the work inside the database. Only the page is read, decoded and sent. A stored sort key (a real column or an index the database can walk) would remove it; that is a change in `fs-objects`.
* `description` is indexed whole for `term`, so a large description is stored twice.
* A task without a title is found by the placeholder `(untitled task)`.
* `TaskItems::list` and `query` still return everything (the in-process frontend uses them); only `page` is cut in the store.

## 0.0.7 review follow-up (open points and limits)

* **Import of many tasks is judged once** (`repo/unblocked_batch.rs`): from 21 created tasks in one flush on, "who became ready" is one scan of the store, readiness judged without the created tasks and with them, the difference announced (plus a created task that waited only for another created one, as when each is asked alone). Before, each task named by a `DEPENDS-ON` cost a scan (quadratic): a `DEPENDS-ON` chain of 300 imported in 35 s in a debug build (`tests/perf_import_depends.rs`), the 1500 chain of `plan_scale` did not finish in 10 minutes (700: 264 s; the review gave 138 s for 1500 in a release build, not measured again here). Now: 300 chain 2.4 s debug, the 1500 chain test of `plan_scale` 16 s debug, 5000 chain import 2.0 s release. Changes queued by edits are still judged one by one (small by nature).
* **Bounds** (`model/actor.rs`): actor IRI at most 2048 bytes and not empty, actor name at most 200 bytes (`assign_actor` and `claim`, refused as a conflict), the end of a claim at most 24 hours ahead (as in `fs-interfaces` v0.0.20). An expired claim can be taken over by another (`tests/actors.rs`).
* **Open: `claim` and `release` trust `by`.** The caller names who claims; nothing binds it to an identity. Harmless today (`task.claim` does not go over the socket, `wire_caps.rs`); it must be tied to the caller's identity when the claim is released onto the socket.
* **Open: boards have no limits** on the number of columns (a sensible bound would be 32), the cards in `order` per place, or the length of names and uids; `board_layout_inner` is O(placed cards × tasks). Order is a vector index, no fractional index. To be added with constants and tests before boards go onto the wire.
* The settings file is synced to disk before it replaces the old one; a relative `$XDG_DATA_HOME` is ignored (`face.rs`, `data_dir_in`).
